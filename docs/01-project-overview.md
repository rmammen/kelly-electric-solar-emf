# Project Overview

## Context

Kelly Electric Company, supported by a Maryland Energy Administration (MEA) grant, was studying whether solar arrays installed within or near utility rights-of-way perform differently than arrays outside them — specifically, whether electromagnetic fields (EMFs) from high-voltage lines have a measurable effect on solar generation.

Our INST490 capstone team was brought in to support the data-analysis side of that study.

## The Research Question

If solar panels at different sites are all oriented identically:
- Is there variation in solar production across the sites?
- Can any variation be linked to EMF exposure within the utility right-of-way?
- If variation exists, is there a measurable correlation between EMF strength and solar productivity?

## Study Design

Three test sites, each with 4 panels:

| Site | Location | Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Site 1 | BGE property | High |
| Site 2 | Edge of the utility right-of-way, under a high-voltage line | Moderate |
| Site 3 | Control site, ~100 yards outside the right-of-way (Milk Association land) | None (control) |

Two data streams were planned for comparison:
- **Solar generation data**, via Victron/VRM monitoring
- **EMF strength data**, from a separate measurement source

The Kelly Electric–MEA contract ran through June 30, 2026, with solar-generation data collection limited to a 3-month window within that period.

## Our Team's Intended Role

- Gather the solar-generation and EMF data
- Tabulate it clearly
- Present it in a way that was easy for stakeholders to understand
- Keep the reporting technically accurate and scientifically valid
