# Kelly Electric Solar & EMF Study
### Client-Facing Project Management Case Study, Spring 2026

## Overview

Our team partnered with **Kelly Electric Company** on a study investigating whether electromagnetic fields (EMFs) from high-voltage utility lines affect the performance of nearby solar arrays. The study used three solar-array test sites in Prince George's County, Maryland — one on BGE property, one at the edge of a utility right-of-way beneath a high-voltage line, and one control site roughly 100 yards outside the right-of-way — each instrumented with 4 panels, comparing solar-generation data against EMF measurements over a multi-month collection window tied to a Maryland Energy Administration (MEA) grant.

This repository documents my individual **project management and stakeholder-liaison contributions** to that project. Client-sensitive and proprietary information has been excluded.

## My Role: Project Manager / Team Liaison

I was formally designated team liaison on **February 16, 2026**, before the team even had a primary point of contact at Kelly Electric. From there, I became the team's central point of coordination for the rest of the semester.

**What that involved:**
- Establishing and maintaining stakeholder communication across Kelly Electric, solar advocates, and county-level contacts
- Organizing and running stakeholder meetings, and following up when responses stalled
- Maintaining a running weekly project log — decisions, blockers, action items, and next steps ([see the full log](docs/project-management-log.md))
- Identifying when the team was being asked to take on work outside its defined scope, and raising that with stakeholders
- Tracking a grant-driven data-collection deadline and repeatedly pushing on dependencies (site installation, system access, data availability) that put it at risk
- Helping the team pivot the final deliverable when the original analysis became infeasible within the semester

## The Situation

When our team was assigned to the project, the analytical goal was clear, but very little of the groundwork needed to pursue it existed yet. There was no established point of contact, stakeholders hadn't met in some time, and data collection hadn't started. I took ownership of getting that moving.

## What I Did

**1. Restarted stakeholder communication.** After being named liaison, I helped get the first stakeholder meeting scheduled and pushed to establish Derek Newman as Kelly Electric's primary contact. Our team's outreach was what re-started a stakeholder relationship that had gone quiet — see [Stakeholder Management](docs/02-stakeholder-management.md).

**2. Managed scope as it shifted.** By early March the team was informed our role was data analysis only — site-selection and broader project goals belonged to the solar advocates (David Brosch, Steve Skolnik, Albert Nunez). When the team was later pulled into site-selection–adjacent work, I flagged that it was outside our defined scope. See [Scope Management](docs/03-scope-management.md).

**3. Tracked and escalated data-access blockers.** The MEA grant required preliminary data collection to begin by March 31. Getting there involved chasing site installation, remote-access software (TeamViewer → Splashtop → VRM portal), and a Kelly Electric contact who became hard to reach. I kept pushing on each of these instead of waiting for them to resolve on their own. See [Data Access & Blockers](docs/04-data-access-and-blockers.md).

**4. Helped the team adapt the deliverable.** By late April, with EMF data still delayed and too many collection/access obstacles to complete the intended analysis in the time remaining, the team redirected the final deliverable toward documenting groundwork, structure, and a statement of work for future capstone teams. See [Project Timeline](docs/05-project-timeline.md).

## Key Takeaway

This project taught me that project management often means creating structure and momentum where none exists yet — not just keeping an existing plan on schedule. A meaningful part of the job was noticing when something was stalled or off-scope, saying so, and adjusting the plan (and the deliverable) when the constraints changed, rather than forcing the original plan to work anyway. See [Lessons Learned](docs/06-lessons-learned.md).
